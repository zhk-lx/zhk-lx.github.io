To date, experimental development biology still faces challenges to efficiently model the morphogenesis of 
cross-species vertebrates, spanning from the embryonic development to postnatal growth. No effective 
computational methods are yet available to reproduce this complex and long-term process. However, recent 
advancements in large-scale computation and multi-voxel assembly theory have created unprecedented opportunities for establishing a virtual platform to simulate these morphomechanical processes. In this context, 
we propose a spatiotemporal topological evolution (STTE) method that can investigate the morphogenesis 
and postnatal growth of three-dimensional multi-voxel virtual embryoids. In this model, the optimization 
goal is to achieve extensive motion capabilities for the heads of virtual organisms in the vertical direction, 
thereby facilitating the formation and maintenance of conserved morphological structures characteristic of 
vertebrates. This proposed theory can rapidly and massively generate digital vertebrates with diverse adult 
body shapes, and this virtual morphogenesis pipeline offers novel perspectives and technologies for developmental biology.
